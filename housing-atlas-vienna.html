    About this analysis

    This view maps the distribution and scale of social municipal housing (Gemeindebau) across Vienna's districts, tracing how the city's housing model has evolved and where new development is concentrated.

    01

    Temporal Dimension

    Pre-1918

    Imperial Vienna

    Voting rights tied to minimum tax requirements, benefiting the wealthy and disenfranchising most of the working class. Tuberculosis spreads aggressively through poor housing — tenement-slums, shack-communities, and workers subletting a mattress for a few hours a day.

    1919–1934

    Red Vienna & Palaces for the Workers

    1919

    End of WWI, fall of the Habsburg Monarchy, Republic of Austria proclaimed. Social Democratic Party wins Vienna — start of "Red Vienna".

    1922

    Vienna becomes a federal state with fiscal sovereignty.

    1923

    "Breitner Tax" (housing & luxury tax) introduced. First municipal programme: 25,000 flats in five years.

    1925

    Metzleinstalerhof completed — first municipal housing estate.

    1927

    Second municipal housing programme initiated.

    1930

    Karl-Marx-Hof superblock completed.

    1933–1945

    Austro-Fascism, National Socialism & the Second World War

    1933

    Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuß exploits a political crisis to suspend democracy and ban opposition parties.

    1934

    Austrian Civil War. Social Democratic Party banned — start of the Austro-Fascist, then National Socialist (Anschluss) era. Municipal housing construction nearly ceases.

    1945

    The Second World War ends. Second Austrian Republic formed, Social Democrats return to power in Vienna. Over 20% of housing stock destroyed; priority on rapid rebuilding.

    1945–1970s

    Post-War Reconstruction & Functionalism

    1951

    With Swedish financial aid, Per-Albin-Hansson-Siedlung — the first postwar municipal estate — is completed.

    1954

    Housing Promotion Act (Wohnbauförderungsgesetz) passed, channelling public funds and levies into municipal/cooperative housing.

    1969

    The 100,000th municipal flat is completed.

    1971

    Großfeldsiedlung completed — Vienna's largest municipal estate, 5,500+ units.

    1970s–2000s

    Market Regulation, Subsidised Housing & the Smart City

    1979

    Limited-Profit Housing Act (WGG) passed — caps rents at cost, requires surplus reinvested into affordable homes.

    1984

    Wohnfonds Wien founded to procure and stockpile real estate for affordable housing.

    1995

    Austria joins the European Union.

    2004

    Last municipal estate completed; subsidised housing shifts entirely to non-profit/limited-profit developers.

    2010s–Today

    Renaissance of Municipal Housing

    2015

    Gemeindebau NEU announced, ending the 11-year freeze on direct municipal construction.

    2019

    Barbara-Prammer-Hof completed — first of the new generation of municipal housing.

    2026

    Allocation System Reform (Wohnungsvergabe NEU) and increased Housing Contribution Tax. 4,900 residents now live in Gemeindebau NEU homes.
